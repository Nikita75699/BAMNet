### Reviewer 1

#### 1. Pixel-to-millimeter conversion and clinical sufficiency

**Manuscript changes:** `bamnet_frontiers.tex`, lines 55, 272-276, 335, 346, and 399-400; `frontiers_SupplementaryMaterial.tex`, lines 59-70 and 75-88.

**Response:** We thank the reviewer for this important suggestion. We have implemented an image-specific pixel-to-millimeter conversion for landmark errors using DICOM metadata rescaled to the $640 \times 640$ model space. The abstract and results now present fold-weighted millimeter metrics: a median error of 2.03 mm and a mean error of 2.66 mm. Detailed landmark-specific and per-fold results in millimeters have been added to Supplementary Tables S1 and S2. We have also revised the clinical discussion to clarify that the model's accuracy is suitable for intraoperative anatomical overlays and pre-positioning guidance, but that BAMNet should be considered a clinician-supervised support tool rather than an autonomous valve deployment system.

#### 2. Rationale for AA1, AA2, STJ1, and STJ2

**Manuscript changes:** `bamnet_frontiers.tex`, lines 122-127. Related clinical context appears at lines 70-77.

**Response:** We clarified the anatomical and clinical rationale for the selected landmarks. AA1 and AA2 define the projected annular reference line, which serves as a two-dimensional fluoroscopic surrogate of the virtual annular plane and is relevant for implantation-depth assessment. STJ1 and STJ2 provide an upper-root reference near the coronary ostial level and help define the projected aortic root axis and the relationship between the annular and sinotubular regions. These landmarks were therefore selected because they represent the key two-dimensional geometric structures used during visual TAVI deployment guidance.

#### 3. Inter-observer and intra-observer variability for segmentation and landmarks

**Manuscript changes:** The annotation workflow was clarified at `bamnet_frontiers.tex`, lines 117-120, but no inter-observer or intra-observer variability metrics were added.

**Response:** We fully agree that evaluating annotation reproducibility is essential. Because the clinical dataset was retrospective, all final ground-truth annotations were generated by a cardiovascular surgeon and validated through consensus review with a senior expert. Since independent parallel annotations were not collected, formal inter- or intra-observer variability cannot be calculated for this cohort. We therefore treat this as a limitation of the available annotation protocol and plan to address it in future prospective data collections.

#### 4. Subgroup analysis by projection angle, valve type, image quality, and anatomical overlap

**Manuscript changes:** No direct subgroup analysis was found.

**Response:** We acknowledge that subgroup analysis would enrich the clinical evaluation of the model. However, our retrospective dataset lacks complete and standardized metadata on projection angles, specific valve models, image quality categories, and anatomical overlap. To avoid speculative classification, we did not perform a retrospective subgroup analysis from incomplete metadata. We consider systematic metadata collection a prerequisite for future multicenter validation.

#### 5. Comparison with CT-fluoroscopy fusion guidance

**Manuscript changes:** `bamnet_frontiers.tex`, lines 406-421. Additional background context appears at lines 81-83.

**Response:** We added a direct comparison with CT-fluoroscopy fusion guidance. The revised discussion states that CT-fluoroscopy fusion provides detailed patient-specific three-dimensional anatomical context and is valuable for procedural planning, C-arm angle selection, annular orientation planning, and assessment of coronary ostial obstruction risk. We also discuss its potential limitations, including registration error, respiratory and cardiac motion, guidewire- or device-induced deformation, and discrepancies between preprocedural CT anatomy and the intraoperative fluoroscopic state. BAMNet is presented as a complementary real-time two-dimensional fluoroscopic image-understanding method, not as a replacement for CT-based planning or CT-fluoroscopy fusion.

#### 6. External validation on an independent cohort

**Manuscript changes:** `bamnet_frontiers.tex`, lines 443-445 and 482-483.

**Response:** We agree that external validation is required before broader generalization can be claimed. An independent external cohort was not available for the current revision. We therefore explicitly added this point as a limitation and listed multicenter external validation across different centers, fluoroscopy systems, and valve types as a future direction.

#### 7. Cross-fold ablation or statistical evidence for robustness

**Manuscript changes:** `bamnet_frontiers.tex`, lines 381-385; `frontiers_SupplementaryMaterial.tex`, lines 93-110.

**Response:** We added a numerical ablation table and clarified that all ablation experiments were conducted on the fold-1 fixed hold-out test set. The full BAMNet configuration achieved the best landmark-related metrics in this ablation analysis. A full cross-fold ablation was not feasible because of computational cost. Therefore, we interpret the ablation results as supportive evidence for the proposed design rather than definitive cross-validated proof of each component.

#### 8. Statistical significance testing against baseline models

**Manuscript changes:** `bamnet_frontiers.tex`, lines 281-286; `frontiers_SupplementaryMaterial.tex`, lines 114-135.

**Response:** We added patient-level paired statistical comparisons. Frame-level metrics were first averaged within each patient, and BAMNet was compared with each baseline using two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. P-values were adjusted for multiple comparisons using the Holm-Bonferroni procedure, and paired bootstrap resampling with 10,000 iterations was used to estimate 95% confidence intervals for mean metric differences. The primary p-values are now reported in Supplementary Table S4.

#### 9. Tone down discussion of autonomous TAVI

**Manuscript changes:** `bamnet_frontiers.tex`, lines 399-400, 425-432, and 455-457.

**Response:** We revised the discussion to state that the current study demonstrates real-time image understanding and clinician-supervised visual guidance, not robotic control or autonomous valve deployment. We explicitly state that any future robot-assisted use would require temporal filtering, device tracking, motion compensation, safety constraints, prospective validation, and continuous operator oversight.

**Manuscript changes:** `bamnet_frontiers.tex`, lines 308-314 and 579-586. Qualitative overlay description appears at lines 602-605.

We plan to evaluate predicted AA center, annular-line angle, and annular diameter/length against preoperative planning measurements in a dedicated future study.

#### 4. Inter-reader error not reported

**Manuscript changes:** Annotation workflow clarification at `bamnet_frontiers.tex`, lines 117-120; no inter-reader error metrics were added.

**Response:** We agree that comparing model performance against human variability is an important step toward establishing clinical readiness. In the current retrospective dataset, all final ground-truth annotations were generated by a cardiovascular surgeon and validated through consensus review with a senior expert. The dataset was not designed as a multi-reader reproducibility study, and independent parallel annotations by multiple blinded readers were not collected. Consequently, inter-reader error metrics cannot be computed for this cohort. We therefore treat this as a limitation of the available annotation protocol.

#### 5. P-values for significance with inter-reader annotations

**Manuscript changes:** Baseline statistical testing is described in `bamnet_frontiers.tex`, lines 281-286, and reported in `frontiers_SupplementaryMaterial.tex`, lines 114-135.

**Response:** We agree that comparing model performance against human variability is an important step toward establishing clinical readiness. In the current retrospective dataset, all final ground-truth annotations were generated by a cardiovascular surgeon and validated through consensus review with a senior expert. Because independent parallel annotations by multiple blinded readers were not collected, inter-reader error metrics and corresponding p-values cannot be computed for this cohort. To provide statistical rigor for the available comparisons, we instead performed patient-level paired statistical testing using Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Holm-Bonferroni correction to compare BAMNet against all machine learning baselines, with the results reported in Supplementary Table S4.

#### 6. Cases where the algorithm does not detect landmarks that are visible in ground truth

**Manuscript changes:** `bamnet_frontiers.tex`, lines 277-279 and 332-333.

**Response:** We appreciate this technical question. BAMNet implements heatmap regression followed by a continuous soft-argmax operation, which inherently yields exactly one coordinate pair for each of the four landmarks per forward pass. Therefore, the network does not have an explicit "non-detection" or discrete failure state. Instead, detection degradation is captured continuously via Euclidean distance errors and PCK (Percentage of Correct Keypoints) metrics. We have clarified this architectural behavior in the Methods section of the revised manuscript.

#### 7. Comparison with nnLandmark and nnU-Net

**Manuscript changes:** `bamnet_frontiers.tex`, lines 448-453.

**Response:** We appreciate the suggestion to reference these established medical imaging frameworks. General-purpose frameworks such as nnU-Net for segmentation and nnLandmark-style setups for coordinate regression represent excellent benchmarks. However, they are typically designed as single-task pipelines, whereas BAMNet addresses simultaneous aortic-root segmentation, landmark localization, visibility handling, and real-time inference at 63 FPS in a single unified network. We have expanded the Discussion section to reference these frameworks, noting the lack of a direct multitask benchmark against them under our cross-validation protocol as a current limitation and an interesting direction for future work.

#### 8. Contrast reduction claim requires citation or qualification

**Manuscript changes:** `bamnet_frontiers.tex`, lines 390-394.

**Response:** We agree with the reviewer's caution. We have toned down the text to clarify that a reduction in intraoperative contrast media volume is a clinical hypothesis that requires prospective validation, rather than a proven conclusion of this retrospective image-processing study. The wording has been modified accordingly, and we have added relevant references highlighting the clinical severity of contrast-induced acute kidney injury (CI-AKI) in TAVI cohorts to justify our motivation. We did not add the reviewer-suggested CMR planning citation because that example concerns preoperative planning, whereas the present work focuses on intraoperative fluoroscopic visualization rather than replacing CT-based or CMR-based preprocedural planning.
